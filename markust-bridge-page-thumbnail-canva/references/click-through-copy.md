# Current copy and information-density authority

Applies to new copy and wording revisions. Goal: give an ad visitor a relevant, supported reason to open the actual destination. Do not promise measured CTR improvement.

## One appeal per page

Create a brief table before images/copy: page, primary appeal, source, support detail, visual, destination. Default four standalone alternatives:
1. Ingredient/formula entry point from the video, linked to one supported customer benefit.
2. A specific recognizable problem, answered by a product reason; do not repeat page 1's ingredient list.
3. A different visualizable appeal with a suitable focused pair when available.
4. A complementary benefit, texture/use reason or real evidence; not another wording of the same benefit.

The latest user instruction replaces the older four-benefit overview. Hydration, moisture and water replenishment do not count as three distinct appeals. If fewer appeals are supported, explain the limitation instead of inventing one. Each page retains product identity and its own CTA. Choose structures for these purposes, not by rotating layouts mechanically; usual options are 04/03/05/02, with 01 for equal comparisons.

## Write enough, without repetition

Each page must communicate relevance, benefit, a concrete supported reason/use detail, and the next step. These are jobs, not mandatory separate boxes. Headlines name a clear concern or desired outcome; supporting text adds evidence or practical meaning instead of synonyms. A question must be answered by product support, not another abstract slogan.

Short phrases are preferred: approximately 3–8 headline words and 4–9 words per benefit phrase when meaning allows. Preserve “helps”, “looking” and source qualifications. Ingredient presence does not alone prove its efficacy. Use feature → supported benefit; if constituent effects are unverified, separate ingredient presence from the supported product-level claim. No invented timing, numbers, urgency, reviews, offers or treatment claims.

Useful support is mandatory, word counts are not. For a single appeal, add relevant ingredient/function, texture or usage context rather than unrelated benefits. A paragraph is justified only when it adds meaning. Avoid a headline/face/button-only card, unexplained ingredient lists and conspicuous empty text columns.

| Structure | Required explanation role |
|---|---|
| 01 comparison | Product, one primary benefit and concise distinct supporting reasons around the labeled pair |
| 02 editorial | Lead/quick points and compact explanation do different work; normally 2–4 readable explanation lines if useful |
| 03 question | Concrete answer linking concern, product and reason |
| 04 photo-first | Product/benefit above the lower-left Before inset, useful compact explanation below it; dominant After |
| 05 focused | One matched concern/pair, one supported formula reason; no unrelated benefit list |

## CTA and fit

Use an action plus the actual destination's content: View Serum Details, Explore the Formula, See How to Use It, Read Customer Reviews, or Shop the Serum only when that destination exists. Write freshly; these are patterns, not a fixed phrase bank. Match the entry ad's promise without strengthening it.

For copy-only work, keep fonts/sizes/theme/photo framing; shorten/rephrase and adjust line breaks first. For whitespace additions, create useful readable support using the current body style; do not redesign existing elements. For new layouts, resolve content first, then jointly adjust type, spacing and boxes. Do not inflate headings, shrink body text or repeat claims to fill space. No universal blank-space percentage or fixed gap is a pass/fail rule.

## Acceptance

At intended preview size, identify product, benefit, reason and next step quickly. Check readable support, connected groups, natural framing, no clipping or accidental voids. Page 04 needs explanation both above and below its inset. Across pages, each adds a different reason; repeated product names/CTA are fine. Verify all claims against job evidence and revise defects before the final preview.
